01 / Our approach

BIOLOGY × ELECTRONICS

Biological materials.
Electronic function.

The idea starts with materials derived from biological systems. Our research investigates how their physical behaviour can be engineered into controllable, measurable memory states.

01 / MATERIAL

Bio-derived active materials

Biologically derived materials form the proposed active element of a memory cell. We investigate how their properties can be tuned for information storage.

02 / INTERFACE

Electronic write and read

Electrical interfaces connect the active material to control and readout circuitry. The aim is to set a memory state and measure it through electronic signals.

03 / ARCHITECTURE

From cells to systems

Memory-cell concepts are the starting point. Our longer-term work explores addressable arrays and integration with semiconductor-compatible hardware.

02 / How it works

THE MEMORY CYCLE

A physical state.
A piece of information.

A memory cell stores information through a distinguishable physical state. Our target architecture brings biologically derived materials into the familiar cycle of electronic memory.

  1. 01

    Write

    Apply a controlled electrical signal to set the active material into a target memory state.

  2. 02

    Hold

    Preserve a state long enough for it to represent stored information. Stability and repeatability are central research questions.

  3. 03

    Read

    Measure the cell’s electrical response and distinguish its stored state through readout circuitry.

Our research is focused on making this cycle reliable in experimental memory-cell architectures.

04 / About BioMem

A different starting point
for the future of memory.

↗ Helsinki, Finland

Biological origin.
Hardware ambition.

BioMem is an early-stage deep-tech project working at the intersection of biomaterials, device engineering, and computer architecture.

Based in Helsinki, we are exploring how biologically derived materials can become functional components of electronic memory. Our work spans active-material development, electrical interfaces, and experimental memory-cell architectures.
